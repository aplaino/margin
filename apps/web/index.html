See what they’re building.
Not what they’re typing.

Margin lives in a thin strip on the edge of your screen: a dot for every friend who’s coding right now. Hover, click or press ⌥M and it slides open to show which tool they’re in, the model they’re using and how long they’ve been at it. Move the pointer away and it tucks back in.

Your all-time total across Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode — input, output and cache writes, read from each tool’s own records on your Mac. Cache reads are left out, so the number means something. Only the total ever leaves your Mac.

Privacy

Private by design.

Your code stays on your Mac.

Friends see the tool, the model, how long you’ve been working and your token total. Never your code, prompts, files, folders or branches.

Private repos stay private.

A project is named only when GitHub confirms it’s public. Everything else shows as “private project”.

End-to-end encrypted.

Messages use the Signal protocol. The server only ever holds ciphertext it can’t read, and only until it’s delivered.

You decide what’s shared.

Turn off the tool, model, repo, time or tokens. Share activity with close friends only. Hide a repo entirely.

Invisible, any time.

One switch and you look exactly like you’re offline.

Invite-only.

Friends add you by your GitHub handle, and you choose who to accept.

Themes

Make it yours.

Five themes, each with its own fonts, colours and material — and each in light and dark. Pick an accent colour and it follows you through all of them.

Placement

Put it exactly where you want it.

Drag the strip up or down either edge of your screen, or across to the other side. It shows up on every Space and over full-screen apps, and remembers where you left it.

Margin for Mac.

Early beta. Updates itself as it improves.

Download

Requires macOS 26 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon.

Installing the beta
  1. Open Margin.dmg and drag Margin onto the Applications folder beside it.
  2. Open Margin from Applications. The beta isn’t notarized by Apple yet, so macOS will stop it the first time.
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.
  4. Margin appears as a thin strip on the edge of your screen. Open it to see how it works and sign in with GitHub. If you opened it from Downloads instead, it offers to move itself into Applications.
